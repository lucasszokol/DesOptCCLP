## Outreach and Interview — Problem Brief Confirmation

Our team conducted researcher outreach via **email**, beginning with Dr. Jeonglae Kim on **October 12, 2026**. In his second response on **October 15**, Dr. Kim indicated a scheduling conflict and recommended contacting Dr. Leixin Ma. Following this recommendation, we emailed Dr. Ma and Dr. Pedro Peralta on **October 16**. Although we did not receive a response from Dr. Peralta, Dr. Ma responded that same day and confirmed a Zoom appointment. We interviewed Dr. Ma on **October 19** to discuss computational challenges in physics-based simulations and the potential applications of machine learning. During the interview, we learned that finite element and finite volume simulations involving complex fluid–structure interactions can require substantial computational resources, particularly for large-scale problems. Dr. Ma also discussed limitations of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), including convergence difficulties and insufficient prediction accuracy for complex nonlinear systems. Following the interview, we contacted Vivian Oletty, a researcher in Dr. Ma's group, on October 19 to further investigate a suitable problem for our project.

On **October 20, 2026**, we conducted a follow-up interview with Vivian Oletty, which served as **confirmation of our problem brief**. The discussion identified fluid flow around a stationary circular cylinder as an appropriate starting point, with a moving cylinder and coupled fluid–structure interactions as potential extensions. The stationary-cylinder problem retains the Navier–Stokes equations while avoiding the additional complexity of structural deformation. Vivian also discussed challenges associated with learning oscillatory dynamics, long-term prediction accuracy, and the application of graph neural networks and frequency-domain techniques to physical simulations. Based on these discussions, our team selected two-dimensional incompressible flow around a fixed cylinder to investigate PINN convergence difficulties at different Reynolds numbers. The problem provides a manageable numerical benchmark directly related to the computational and machine-learning limitations identified through our researcher outreach.

### Selected Interview Questions and Answers

| Questions | Answers |
|---|---|
| What computational challenges limit the application of conventional numerical solvers? | Dr. Ma explained that large-scale finite element and finite volume simulations, particularly those involving fluid–structure interactions, can require millions of computational nodes and several hours of computation, even on supercomputers. |
| Have PINNs been successful in addressing these computational challenges? | Dr. Ma indicated that PINNs have been investigated but often experience convergence and accuracy difficulties, especially for problems involving multiple PDEs and geometric nonlinearities. |
| What physical problem would provide a suitable starting point for our project? | Vivian identified fluid flow around a fixed cylinder as an appropriate initial problem, with a moving cylinder as a possible extension. |
| How long might a simulation of the simplified problem take? | Vivian's meeting notes indicate approximately 1–2 hours, potentially less than one hour depending on the setup. |
| What alternative machine-learning approaches have been explored in related research? | Vivian discussed MeshParamNet, which combines graph neural networks with FFT-based representations and uses finite element simulation data to predict structural deformation. |
